## Post-route `repair_design` sizes its buffering on the placement estimate

A post-route round's `repair_design`, the electrical round's included, finds max-slew and max-capacitance violators on the extracted SPEF, but on OpenROAD 26Q4 it sizes the buffers that repair them on Steiner trees at the placement wire RC. A net whose extraction is far above that estimate is left as it is when the estimate is within the limit less the margin, so a larger margin does not help. It can resize a driver that has a stronger variant, which the timer judges on the SPEF, so a driver at its strongest size, such as a `buf_16` driving a long chain, is the usual case. Running the repair on detailed-routing parasitics instead would build its buffer trees from the global routes, which a fresh session rebuilds incompletely (`RSZ-0074`, see [Post-route repair works around OpenROAD routing failures](https://rtl-buddy.github.io/rtl_buddy/dev/known-issues/#post-route-repair-works-around-openroad-routing-failures)).

When the electrical round is kept, its extraction lists each violator still on its SPEF that the estimate judges within the limit less `electrical-repair`'s margin, each at the scene where its extracted violation is worst; rb warns `pnr.electrical_round_unseen` and records them in `post_route_repair.electrical_round.unseen`. The list leaves out the violators `repair_design` does not repair on any parasitics: ports, and pins on constant-driven, clock, dont_touch, special or abutment nets. Raise `post-route-repair.repair-design.wire-rc` toward the RC the extraction shows, or keep such nets short from the start with `placement.repair-design.max-wire-length`. See [Repair electrical violators after routing](https://rtl-buddy.github.io/rtl_buddy/dev/concepts/pnr/#repair-electrical-violators-after-routing).
